Reality
An FDE ships production code inside the client's stack, measured on deployment outcomes - not staffed against a billable-utilisation target.
Myth 02
"Embedded means relocating to the customer's office."
Reality
Embedded is hybrid, not relocation.
Myth 03
"It's entry-level delivery work."
Reality

Reality

Who is an FDE?

Role. Engagement.

<The simplest definition>

An FDE sits where three things overlap

AI Engineer

Agents, RAG, evals, guardrails - you know how AI systems behave, and how they fail in production.

Customer Facing

You can sit with a client, find the real problem behind the stated one, and be trusted inside their environment.

Solutions Architect

You can design a system that fits their constraints, integrations and compliance - not a reference diagram.

Most engineers have one or two. The FDE has all three - that is the whole scarcity.

<What the loop actually looks like>

The rounds that decide it are not a standard SWE loop

5-7 rounds total. Three of them barely exist in a normal software interview - which is exactly why people walk in unprepared.

Round 01
Decomposition

Take an ambiguous customer problem and frame it end to end. Define the KPIs and business outcome first, then the technical path. Palantir-origin; now used at OpenAI, Anthropic, Glean, Databricks.

Round 02
Client simulation

A live stakeholder conversation - scope it, push back, get to alignment. You are being scored on how you handle disagreement, not on being agreeable.

Round 03
AI system design

Defend a production agentic architecture: evals, guardrails, cost ceiling, failure modes. Defend your choices - don't recite a reference architecture.

Coding is lighter than you expect

Some labs add a screen (Google does). Where present, scope is lighter than a SWE loop - and increasingly it's AI-assisted pair coding, judged on how well you collaborate with the model.

Then the one nobody preps for

A procurement and security cross-examination: data residency, audit trail, SLAs, sub-processors, prompt-injection mitigation. Enterprise buyers ask this. So do interviewers.

<Before we map your lane>

What the hiring manager is actually solving for

An FDE hire is a risk decision, not a skills checklist. They are putting you inside a paying customer's environment, largely alone. So they screen for three fears - in this order.

FEAR 01 "Can they build it?"
82%
of FDE job descriptions require AI/ML

Table stakes - and the easiest of the three to prove. Almost everyone in this webinar can close this one.

FEAR 02 "Will they survive the customer?"
99%
customer-facing · 92% embedded

This is the one most engineers have zero evidence for - and the one that quietly kills strong technical candidates.

FEAR 03 "Will it still run after they leave?"
~95%
of pilots died before production

Deployment ownership and clean handover. They have been burned by this before - that is why the role was created.

The bar is not more years - it is evidence across all three. That is why 73% of reqs say mid-senior, yet a 4-year engineer with one deployed system beats a 12-year engineer with none.
